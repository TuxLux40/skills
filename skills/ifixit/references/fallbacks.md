# No guide for the exact model?

Go down the ladder, stop at the first hit, and **say which rung you used** — the user needs to know the guide is approximate.

1. **Name variants** — `suggest` with fewer words, manufacturer series only, or the internal type number (ThinkCentre "Machine Type" e.g. 10RQ/10RR, Pixel codename e.g. `shiba`). Check `children` of the parent device.
2. **Sibling model / same chassis** — e.g. ThinkCentre Tiny generations share chassis across M7xx/M9xx/M6xx of the same year; AMD `q` and Intel models of one generation usually share mechanical design. Flag: board layout, fan, and port positions may differ.
3. **Generic technique guide** — `search "<task>"` with `type: technique` results (thermal paste, SSD replacement in a 2.5"/M.2 slot, keyboard switch replacement). Pair with the device page photos.
4. **Answers forum** — `search "<device> <symptom>" ?filter=question`. Good for symptom diagnosis, weak for step-by-step.
5. **Manufacturer docs** — Lenovo Hardware Maintenance Manual (HMM, `pcsupport.lenovo.com` → Manuals), Dell Service Manual, HP Maintenance & Service Guide, Framework guides, Google Pixel repair manuals (self-service programme). These are often *better* than iFixit for business PCs.
6. **Teardown videos** — last resort; describe them as unverified.

If nothing at all: give a generic, clearly-labelled disassembly *approach* (screws under feet/labels, clip direction, where batteries usually sit), and recommend photographing every layer. No invented specifics.
