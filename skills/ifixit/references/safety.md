# Repair safety

Surface the relevant items **before** step 1, not mid-guide.

## Lithium-ion / LiPo batteries
- Discharge below ~25 % before opening (less energy if punctured).
- Never pry *against* the cell, bend it, or use metal tools on it. Plastic cards / spudgers only.
- Adhesive pull-tabs: pull slowly, low angle. If they snap, use isopropyl (≥90 %) under the cell and wait — don't lever.
- **Swollen battery:** stop. Don't charge, don't compress. Remove only if it can be done without pressure; store in a fireproof spot (metal box, sand) away from flammables.
- Smoke / hiss / heat → move away, ventilate. Don't use water on a large pack burning in the device; a Class D or sand is better, but evacuation beats heroics.
- Disposal (DE): Batterie-Rücknahme at any retailer selling batteries (ElektroG/BattDG), or the Wertstoffhof. Tape the terminals. Never household waste.

## Mains power, PSUs, capacitors
- ATX/SFX PSUs, monitors, TVs, microwaves, CRTs hold dangerous charge after unplugging. Default advice: **don't open PSUs** — replace them.
- If the guide requires it: unplug, wait, discharge per guide with an insulated resistor tool, verify with a multimeter.
- Microwave HV capacitor and CRT anode are potentially lethal — recommend a professional unless the user clearly has training.

## ESD
- Touch grounded metal / use a wrist strap before handling boards, RAM, SSDs.
- Work on a non-carpeted surface; keep parts in anti-static bags.
- Disconnect the battery connector **first** inside phones/laptops before unplugging any other ribbon.

## Heat & adhesive (phones, tablets, some laptops)
- iOpener / heat gun at low setting, ~80 °C on the surface. More heat ≠ faster; it delaminates OLEDs and damages cells.
- Opening breaks the water seal (IP rating). Replacement adhesive needed — list it in Parts.
- Cracked glass: tape it first (packing tape grid) so it lifts as one piece.

## Ribbon cables & connectors
- ZIF connectors: flip the locking flap, don't pull the cable.
- Press-fit connectors: lift straight up with a spudger at the connector edge, never by the cable.
- Photograph each layer before disconnecting.

## Screws
- Different lengths in one assembly are common; a long screw in a short hole can punch through a board/display. Use a magnetic mat or a numbered grid matching guide steps.
- Correct bit matters: JIS vs Phillips on Japanese hardware, Pentalobe on Apple, Torx/tri-wing on many consoles — always check the guide's `tools` list.

## Warranty / legal (EU/DE)
- Opening the device generally doesn't void statutory warranty (Gewährleistung) for unrelated defects, but the seller may argue causation. If still under warranty for the same fault, a claim is usually the better path.
- EU Right-to-Repair rules mean spare parts / manuals are increasingly available from manufacturers — check before buying third-party.
