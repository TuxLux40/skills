# Region: NRW — Rheinland, Ruhrgebiet (Essen, Mülheim an der Ruhr)

Regional layer on top of `quellen-deutschland.md`. Holdings and online
coverage change; check the archive's own page before stating that a specific
year is online. Facts marked *(verify)* were not cross-checked when this file
was written.

## 1. Record layers by side of the Rhine

| Area | c. 1798/1810–1814 | 1815–1874 | from 1 Oct 1874 |
|------|-------------------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Left bank** (Köln left bank, Aachen, Neuss, Krefeld, Kleve left bank, Eifel) | French Zivilstandsregister from 1798 | Civil registers **continue without a break** | Prussian Standesamt |
| **Right bank, former Grand Duchy of Berg** (Düsseldorf, Mülheim an der Ruhr, Essen area, Wuppertal, Bergisches Land) | Zivilstandsregister from 1810 | Mostly back to **church books**, with annual **Kirchenbuchduplikate** handed to the courts; some places kept civil registers longer | Prussian Standesamt |
| **Westfalen / Lippe** | Kgr. Westphalen / Berg registers 1808/1810–1814 where they applied | Church books + Kirchenbuchduplikate 1815–1874 | Prussian Standesamt (Lippe: 1876) |

So for a right-bank family between 1815 and 1874 the path is: church book →
Kirchenbuchduplikat as control copy. A missing civil record in that period is
expected, not negative evidence.

French-period entries: French or German, Republican calendar until end of
1805 (left bank only), ten-year indexes (Dezennaltabellen).

## 2. Where the records are

- **Landesarchiv NRW, Abteilung Rheinland (Duisburg)** — Personenstandsarchiv
  Rheinland: Zivilstandsregister (to 1875) and Kirchenbuchduplikate of the
  Rhine province, second copies of the Standesamt registers; much digitised
  on archive.nrw.de. Finding aid: "Die Zivilstandsregister und die
  Kirchenbuchduplikate im Personenstandsarchiv Rheinland. Eine Übersicht".
- **Landesarchiv NRW, Abteilung Ostwestfalen-Lippe (Detmold)** — the same for
  all of Westfalen and Lippe; death second-registers 1874–1938 and marriage
  second-registers to 1899 were digitised with FamilySearch.
- **Municipal archives** hold the first copies of Standesamt registers once
  the access periods (110/80/30) have run.

### Essen

- **Haus der Essener Geschichte / Stadtarchiv**: Standesamt registers start
  **1 Oct 1874**; exception **Mintard**, where Zivilstandsregister exist from
  1810. Yearly name indexes, partly digitised, searchable in the archive's
  online database; extracts are ordered from the archive for a flat fee per
  entry.
- Many of today's districts were separate municipalities with their own
  Standesamt (Altenessen, Borbeck, Steele, Werden, Kettwig, Kray, …) —
  identify the Standesamt at the date of the event, not "Essen".
- **Catholic**: the area belonged to the Erzbistum Köln (parts to Münster and
  Paderborn) until the **Bistum Essen** was founded in 1958 *(verify)*. Church
  books: Archiv des Bistums Essen, about 1,000 volumes digitised in its own
  church-book portal and linked from **Matricula** (free).
- **Protestant**: Evangelische Kirche im Rheinland — **Archion**.
- Before 1802/03 Essen was an imperial abbey (Stift Essen), Werden an imperial
  abbey of its own *(verify)*: non-church records of that period sit in
  Landesarchiv NRW Abt. Rheinland under those territories.

### Mülheim an der Ruhr

- **Stadtarchiv Mülheim an der Ruhr** (holdings list for family research):
  - Zivilstandsregister of the Munizipalität Mülheim **1810–1819**
  - Standesamt: births 1874–1915, marriages 1874–1945, deaths 1874–1995,
    **Sammelakten** from 1874
  - Second registers online via Landesarchiv NRW: marriages 1874–1899,
    deaths 1874–1938
  - Church-book compilations (Geschichtsverein): Reformed 1658–1839, Lutheran
    1718–1810, Catholic St. Mariae Geburt 1751–1810, Catholic Saarn
    1707–1809, Jewish community 1811–1874 — *compilations, i.e. derivative*;
    go to the original for proof
  - Address books 1814–1999, 1860–1978 online
- Mülheim was predominantly Reformed (Herrschaft Broich) *(verify)* — check
  Reformed, Lutheran and Catholic books separately for the same family.

## 4. Ruhr-specific patterns

- **Mass in-migration from c. 1850**: families in Essen/Mülheim often arrive
  from the Bergisches Land, Westfalen, Hessen, the eastern Prussian provinces
  (Ost-/Westpreußen, Posen, Schlesien — "Ruhrpolen", Masuren), the
  Netherlands and Austria-Hungary. The birthplace in a Standesamt entry is
  the bridge — and the usual place where trees graft the wrong family.
- **Places of the same or similar name in the east** (and Germanised/Polish
  variants): resolve with Meyers Gazetteer + GOV before searching.
- **Sources for miners and workers**: address books (yearly residence and
  occupation), Standesamt Sammelakten, company and Knappschaft records
  (Bergbau-Archiv Bochum, company archives such as Krupp) *(verify holdings
  before citing)*.
- **Frequent moves** within the conurbation: each child may be registered in a
  different Standesamt. Reconstruct the family group through address books.
- **Dutch-influenced names on the Lower Rhine** (van/ter/ten, patronymics
  such as Janssen, Peters, Hendricks) — see `land-niederlande.md`.
