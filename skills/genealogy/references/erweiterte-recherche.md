# Erweiterte Recherche / Advanced research tactics

For brick walls, for events abroad, and for finding people who hold
information. Everything found this way enters the normal pipeline: citation,
classification, status.

## 1. Principle: follow the paper an event produced

Every event in a life made some office or organisation write something down.
Ask in this order:

1. **What happened, when, where** — as narrowly as family papers, photos
   (uniforms, studio stamps, backgrounds), letters and interviews allow.
2. **Who recorded it at the time** — in the home country, in the other
   country, and at international bodies. Think beyond registers: employer,
   military unit, camp, shipping line, church, court, hospital, school,
   insurance, union, club, newspaper.
3. **Where those records are today** — successor authority, state or
   national archive, organisation archive; and which finding aid lists them.
4. **Request in the language and form that repository expects**, with the
   identifiers it indexes by (often not the name: service number, prisoner
   number, ship and date, case number).
5. **Cross-check with a second, independent record series** from the other
   side (home-country record against host-country record).

Research the responsible bodies fresh each time; do not assume. If a country
comes up repeatedly, write a short country reference in the style of
`land-niederlande.md` and add it to the skill's table.

### Worked pattern: an event abroad (military captivity as the example)

| Step | Question | Typical holders |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| Home side | Service record, unit, date and place of capture, release | National military personnel archive, tracing services, discharge papers in the family |
| Detaining power | Prisoner personnel file, camp rosters, transfers, labour assignments | That country's national archives, military record centres |
| International | Capture cards, camp inspection reports | Red Cross archives |
| Local | Camp history, work details, local press, cemetery | State/county historical societies, local newspapers, museum collections at the camp site |
| Family | Letters with camp postmarks and prisoner numbers, release certificate, photos | Relatives — ask in interviews |

The same grid works for emigration (departure lists ↔ arrival lists ↔
naturalisation), labour migration, flight and expulsion, colonial or
merchant service, study or religious life abroad.

## 2. Source types beyond the registers

- **Newspapers**: family notices, court reports, local news, casualty lists,
  emigration and ship notices. Search OCR with variants; read the page image.
- **Address and telephone books**: yearly residence and occupation; trace a
  person year by year to bracket moves, marriages, deaths.
- **Cemeteries**: grave registers, gravestone projects, burial-plot owners
  (often a relative).
- **Maps, cadastre, house books**: who lived next to whom.
- **Membership and occupational records**: guilds, miners' funds, unions,
  clubs, veterans' associations, school and university registers.
- **Court, notary, inheritance and guardianship files**: name heirs and
  relationships explicitly.
- **Lists made in passing**: passenger, hospital, tax, conscription, voter,
  welfare, refugee and housing lists.
- **Published compilations**: local histories, Ortsfamilienbücher,
  Festschriften, church and company chronicles — leads with footnotes to
  follow.

## 3. Search technique

- **Variant matrix**: every spelling, Rufname, Latin/translated form,
  transliteration, maiden and married name, initials; reversed name order.
- **Search around the person** (cluster/FAN): siblings, in-laws, godparents,
  witnesses, neighbours, fellow emigrants from the village, comrades of the
  same unit. Rare surnames in the cluster are easier to find than a common
  target name.
- **Search by something other than the name**: address, farm, occupation +
  place, ship + date, unit, parish + year.
- **Operators**: exact phrase, exclusion, site restriction, date range,
  proximity ("Vorname" near "Nachname") where the search engine offers it.
- **Language**: search in the language and script of the place and period,
  with the place name as used then.
- **Images**: reverse image search for studio portraits and postcards;
  photographer's imprint and address dates a photo via address books.
- **Web archives**: dead links and vanished society pages often survive in
  the Internet Archive.
- **Ask people**: regional mailing lists and forums, the local Heimat- or
  history society, the parish office, the archive's own staff. One informed
  local saves weeks.
- Log every search (engine, query, date, result) like any other search.

## 4. Finding people

### Deceased persons

No restriction of method: all of the above, plus obituaries, probate,
directories, memorial sites, online trees as leads.

### Living persons

Purpose: **establish how they are related, and reach them** — distant
cousins, DNA matches, descendants of an emigrant branch, of neighbours,
comrades or fellow prisoners, keepers of family papers.

- Work **downwards from documented ancestors** (descendancy research):
  obituaries naming survivors, marriage and family notices, public trees,
  DNA-match trees, society member lists.
- Use the contact channel the person offers themselves: the tree site's
  message system, the DNA match inbox, a public profile's contact function,
  a society or a known mutual relative as intermediary.
- First message: who you are, the common ancestor with dates and place,
  what you can share, one concrete question; no pressure, no surprises about
  sensitive findings in a first message. Template in `assets/vorlagen.md`.
- Out of scope for this skill: compiling profiles of living people
  (addresses, employers, daily life, household). It is not needed to
  establish kinship or make contact.
- No answer is an answer for now; one polite follow-up after some weeks,
  then leave it.

## 5. Brick-wall checklist

1. Is the identity of the last proven person actually proven?
2. Whole family group reconstructed, incl. second marriages?
3. Every record type for that place and period checked — and logged?
4. Neighbouring parishes, other confession, garrison/military parish?
5. Place resolved correctly at that date? Same-named places excluded?
6. Name variants, patronymic or farm-name change considered?
7. Records created at *later* events (marriage of children, death, estate)?
8. Cluster searched (godparents, witnesses, neighbours)?
9. Indirect evidence assembled and written up as a hypothesis?
10. DNA: any cluster pointing at this couple? (`dna-myheritage.md`)
