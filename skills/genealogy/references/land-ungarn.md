# Ungarn / Hungary — short reference

For lines from the Kingdom of Hungary (incl. Danube Swabians and migrants to
the Ruhr). Method is unchanged; this lists what differs. Items marked
*(verify)* were not cross-checked when this file was written.

## Record layers

| Period | Record | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| from **1 Oct 1895** | State civil registration (állami anyakönyv), Act 23 of 1894 | Originals at the civil registry office, copies in the county archives. Forms changed in 1907. Legal access restrictions apply to recent entries |
| before 1 Oct 1895 | **Church registers** (egyházi anyakönyvek) of the Roman Catholic, Greek Catholic, Orthodox, Reformed (Calvinist), Lutheran and Jewish communities | The church register *was* the civil record |
| from 1828 | **Duplicates** of church registers deposited with the authorities | Now in the competent county/municipal archives; control copy where the original is lost |

## Where to search

- **FamilySearch** — filmed church registers to 1895 ("Hungary, Catholic
  Church Records", Reformed, Lutheran, Jewish collections) and "Hungary,
  Civil Registration, 1895–1980"; coverage varies by locality, many films are
  browse-only.
- **Magyar Nemzeti Levéltár** (National Archives of Hungary, mnl.gov.hu) —
  microfilm collection of the pre-1895 registers for present-day Hungary and
  some border regions (Burgenland, Csallóköz, Bácska, Mura region, southern
  Baranya).
- **Hungaricana**, **MACSE** (Hungarian Society for Family History Research)
  — databases and indexes *(verify current offer)*.
- **Gazetteers**: the 1895 gazetteer of the lands of the Hungarian Crown;
  Lipszky, *Repertorium locorum* (1808) for older name forms.

## Pitfalls

- **Trianon 1920**: two thirds of the historical kingdom lie outside today's
  Hungary. Records of places now in **Slovakia, Romania (Transylvania,
  Banat), Serbia (Vojvodina), Croatia, Austria (Burgenland), Ukraine
  (Transcarpathia), Slovenia** are in those countries' archives. Establish
  the present-day country before planning.
- **Place names in several languages** — Hungarian, German, Slovak, Romanian,
  Serbian, Latin (Pozsony / Pressburg / Bratislava; Temesvár / Temeschburg /
  Timișoara). Many villages shared one name. Record the name as written plus
  the modern name and county (vármegye/megye).
- **Name order**: Hungarian puts the surname first (Tóth József). Indexes and
  trees regularly swap the two.
- **Married women**: "Tóth Józsefné" means "wife of József Tóth" and hides
  her own name entirely.
- **Magyarisation of names** (névmagyarosítás), especially late 19th century
  to the 1930s: Schmidt → Kovács, Klein → Kis, Müller → Molnár. A surname
  change within one person's life is common, and it was officially recorded
  *(verify where the name-change registers are held)*.
- **Language of the registers**: Latin in Catholic books until the 1840s,
  then Hungarian; German in German-settled parishes; Church Slavonic in
  Orthodox ones *(verify dates locally)*.
- **Given names translate**: János/Johann/Joannes, István/Stephan,
  Ferenc/Franz, György/Georg, Erzsébet/Elisabeth, Katalin/Katharina,
  Ilona/Helene.
- **Danube Swabians (Donauschwaben)**: 18th-century settlers from south-west
  Germany. The link back to the German village of origin is the classic
  graft point — demand a settlement or church record naming the origin.
  Many villages have an Ortsfamilienbuch (derivative; verify).
- **Calendar**: Gregorian throughout for the Western churches; Orthodox
  parishes kept the Julian calendar *(verify per parish)*.
