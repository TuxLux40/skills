# Niederlande / Netherlands — short reference

For lines crossing the Dutch–German border (Lower Rhine, Gelderland, Limburg,
East Frisia–Groningen). Method is unchanged; this lists what differs. Items
marked *(verify)* were not cross-checked when this file was written.

## Record layers

| Period | Record | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| from **1811** (Limburg and other southern parts from **1796**) | **Burgerlijke Stand** — geboorte-, huwelijks-, overlijdensakten | Ten-year indexes (tienjarige tafels) are dated by the day of *registration*, not of the event. Marriage entries come with **huwelijksbijlagen** (supporting documents: birth extracts, parents' death records) |
| before 1811 | **DTB** — Doop-, Trouw- en Begraafboeken (church registers) | Confiscated by the state in 1811 and therefore largely in public archives |
| from c. 1850 | **Bevolkingsregister** (population register) | Whole households with moves in and out — the tool for following a family between municipalities *(verify start year per municipality)* |

**Public access:** births after 100 years, marriages and huwelijksbijlagen
after 75, deaths after 50.

## Where to search

- **WieWasWie** (wiewaswie.nl) — national index of civil-status and other
  records, links to scans at the holding archive.
- **Open Archieven** (openarch.nl) — aggregated index of Dutch and Belgian
  archives, free *(verify coverage)*.
- Provincial portals and archives: e.g. Historisch Centrum Limburg,
  AlleFriezen, Gelders Archief, Nationaal Archief (Zuid-Holland).
- Scans are usually free; civil-status registers older than 100 years can be
  digitised on request at some archives.
- **CBG – Centrum voor familiegeschiedenis** for 20th-century personal record
  cards of deceased persons *(verify conditions)*.
- **Delpher** — digitised newspapers and books *(verify)*.

## Pitfalls

- **Surname adoption 1811** (naamsaanneming): before it, patronymics were
  common in the north and east (Jans, Hendriks, Pieters); the adopted surname
  may have no earlier history. Registers of name adoption exist per
  municipality *(verify survival locally)*.
- **Prefixes** (van, van der, de, ter, ten) are written lower-case and
  ignored in Dutch alphabetical order: "van den Berg" sorts under B. German
  clerks ran them together or dropped them (Vandenberg, Terhorst).
- **Married women keep their maiden name** in records, as in German Catholic
  registers.
- **Name forms across the border**: Jan/Johann, Hendrik/Heinrich,
  Willem/Wilhelm, Gerrit/Gerhard, Derk/Dirk/Dietrich, Trijntje/Catharina,
  Geertruida/Gertrud, Aaltje/Adelheid. A spelling change at the border is not
  a conflict.
- **Language**: French-period entries 1811–1813 may be in French.
- **Confession**: Reformed (hervormd), Catholic, Mennonite (doopsgezind —
  adult baptism, so no infant baptism entry), Lutheran, Jewish — check which
  congregation's DTB applies.
- The Lower Rhine (Kleve, Geldern, Moers) was administratively and
  linguistically close to the Netherlands; Dutch-language church books on
  the German side are normal before the 19th century.
