# Interviews / Mündliche Überlieferung (oral history)

Relatives are the only source that disappears. Interview early; verify later.

## 1. Preparation

- From the tree, list for this person: what they could know first-hand
  (own life, parents, grandparents, siblings, neighbours), which gaps and
  conflicts in the tree touch that, which photos and documents are
  unidentified.
- Bring prompts: photos, a simple chart, documents, maps of the place.
- Agree beforehand: recording yes/no, who may hear or read it, what happens
  to it. Record the consent at the start of the tape and note it in the
  file. Respect "not on tape" and "don't write that down".
- One person at a time where possible; 60–90 minutes; quiet room; test the
  recorder.

## 2. Conducting

- Start with easy, concrete ground (childhood home, school, work).
- **Open questions**, one at a time: "Wie war …?", "Erzähl mir von …",
  "Woran erinnerst du dich bei …?". No leading questions, no suggested
  names or dates.
- Let silences stand. Do not correct. Ask "Woher weißt du das?" — the
  answer decides whether a statement is eyewitness or hearsay.
- Pin loose statements to anchors: "Wart ihr da schon umgezogen?", "War
  das vor oder nach dem Krieg?", "Wer war noch dabei?".
- For photos: who, where, when, who took it, who owned it — write the
  answer against the photo's file name, not on the print.
- Close with: "Wen sollte ich noch fragen?", "Gibt es Unterlagen, Briefe,
  ein Familienbuch?", and permission to come back.

Topic list to draw from (not a script): names incl. nicknames and maiden
names; places and addresses; dates of family events; occupations and
employers; religion and parish; military service, captivity, flight,
expulsion, emigration; illnesses and causes of death as far as offered;
family stories and their source; quarrels and breaks; who kept the papers;
relatives abroad.

## 3. Transcription

No tool is prescribed. Requirements, whatever is used:

- Time stamps at least per paragraph; speakers marked.
- Verbatim for names, places, dates and dialect terms; filler words may be
  dropped; nothing is "corrected".
- A machine transcript is **provisional**: names, places and years are
  checked against the audio by the user before anything is taken from it.
- Recordings of living relatives are personal; where the transcription runs
  is the user's call.

## 4. Filing

Keep together, same base name (see `zitieren-und-dokumentation.md`):

```
<JJJJ-MM-TT>_Interview_<NACHNAME-Vorname>_<Ort>.<wav|flac|mp3>   master audio, never edited
…_transkript.md        transcript with time stamps and status
…_auswertung.md        extracted statements (below)
…_einwilligung.md      what was agreed about use
```

## 5. Evaluation

Split the interview into single statements and classify each:

| Aussage | Zeitmarke | Woher weiß sie/er es | Information | Prüfbar durch | Status |
|---------|-----------|----------------------|-------------|---------------|--------|
| "Opa kam 1947 aus Gefangenschaft zurück" | 00:14:10 | selbst erlebt als Kind | primär, aus großer zeitlicher Distanz | Entlassungsschein, Melderegister | möglich |

- Own experience = primary information, weakened by distance in time;
  everything "man hat erzählt" = secondary.
- Typical distortions: telescoped dates, merged persons of the same name,
  embellished origins (nobility, "three brothers"), silence about
  illegitimacy, party membership, crimes, suicide. A family story is a lead
  with a kernel worth testing — not a fact and not a lie.
- Every checkable statement becomes a research question with a named
  source. Uncheckable statements stay in the interview file, cited as oral
  information.
- Citation: `Mündliche Auskunft von <Name>, <Verhältnis>, Gespräch am
  <Datum> in <Ort>, Aufnahme und Transkript im Besitz d. Verf., <Zeitmarke>`.
- Two relatives telling the same story are independent only if they did not
  get it from the same person.
