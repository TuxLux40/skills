# Quellen in Deutschland / Sources for German research

Planning aid: which records exist, who holds them, how to get at them. Portal
contents, fees and URLs change — verify current coverage on the portal itself
before telling the user a specific volume is online. Do not invent a Signatur.

## 1. Work backwards through the record layers

| Period | Main source | Held by |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| Living memory | Relatives, family papers, Familienstammbuch, photos, Totenzettel | Family — interview first, record who said what and when |
| 1876 → today (earlier in some territories, see below) | **Personenstandsregister** (Geburt, Heirat, Tod) | Standesamt while within access periods; afterwards the municipal/district archive (Stadt-/Kreisarchiv) or state archive |
| ~1550/1650 → 1875 | **Kirchenbücher** (Taufen, Trauungen, Bestattungen; also Konfirmanden, Kommunikanten, Familienregister) | Parish, church archive (landeskirchliches Archiv, Bistumsarchiv), partly state archives |
| Before / beside church books | Steuerlisten, Schatzungsregister, Lagerbücher, Bürgerbücher, Gerichts- und Notariatsakten, Lehns- und Hofakten, Leichenpredigten | State and municipal archives |

## 2. Civil registration (Standesamt)

- **Reich-wide from 1 Jan 1876**; Prussia from 1 Oct 1874.
- **Earlier under French law:** left bank of the Rhine from 1798 (kept until
  1875); Grand Duchy of Berg (right bank, e.g. Düsseldorf) from 1810; Kingdom
  of Westphalia from 1808/1810; Hanseatic departments from 1811. After 1813/14
  most right-bank territories went back to church registration, while Bremen
  and Lübeck continued their civil registers. Baden introduced state civil
  registration on 1 Feb 1870. Other states and free cities had their own
  systems — check GenWiki "Personenstandsregister" and the territory's article
  for exact dates.
- **Access periods (PStG, since 2009):** births 110 years, marriages 80 years,
  deaths 30 years after the entry. Within the period (§ 62 PStG): the person
  concerned, spouse/partner, ancestors and descendants; anyone else must show
  a legal interest (rechtliches Interesse). For **birth and death** registers
  a sibling only needs a justified interest (berechtigtes Interesse). A
  justified interest also suffices for anyone once 30 years have passed since
  the death of the last participant (parents and child for births, the
  spouses for marriages). After the period the registers become archive
  material and are open under the respective Archivgesetz.
- **What to order:** a copy of the full register entry (*beglaubigte
  Registerabschrift* / *Kopie des Registereintrags mit Randvermerken*), not a
  short Urkunde — only the full entry shows informant, witnesses, occupations,
  addresses and marginal notes.
- **Sammelakten** (supporting files to marriages) can contain parents' death
  certificates, birth extracts, consent — ask whether they survive.
- **Standesamt I in Berlin** holds surviving registers of former eastern
  territories and of Germans abroad; many eastern registers are in Polish state
  archives or lost.
- French-period entries are in French (later German), dated in the Republican
  calendar until end of 1805; they come with Dezennaltabellen (ten-year
  indexes), which are the fastest finding aid.

## 3. Church books

- **Protestant:** *Archion* (archion.de, paid) — portal of the Protestant
  church archives; coverage by Landeskirche varies.
- **Catholic:** *Matricula* (matricula-online.eu, free) — many German dioceses,
  plus Austria, Luxembourg, parts of Poland etc. Dioceses not on Matricula:
  ask the Bistumsarchiv (reading room or own portal).
- **FamilySearch** — images and indexes of many filmed church books and
  duplicates; some viewable only in affiliate libraries or FS centres.
- **Ancestry / MyHeritage** — indexed collections, often derived from the same
  films; use as finding aids and go to the image.
- **Kirchenbuchduplikate/Zweitschriften** in state archives fill gaps where the
  parish original is lost (and vice versa).
- Other confessions: Jewish registers (state archives; much lost), Mennonite,
  Huguenot/Walloon and military parishes (Militärkirchenbücher) have separate
  series — ask where the regiment's or congregation's books went.
- First find **which parish served the village at that date** (Filialkirche,
  eingepfarrt nach …) — GOV and Meyers Gazetteer state it.

## 4. CompGen / GenWiki ecosystem (free)

Verein für Computergenealogie — the central German-language infrastructure:

- **GenWiki** (wiki.genealogy.net) — regional portals, parish and archive
  articles, record-type explanations. First stop for "what exists for place X".
- **GOV** — Geschichtliches Orts-Verzeichnis (gov.genealogy.net): places with
  stable IDs, administrative and parish affiliations over time. Record the
  GOV-ID with every place.
- **GEDBAS** — user-submitted trees (lead quality, like any tree).
- **Online-OFB** (ofb.genealogy.net) — Ortsfamilienbücher; plus the
  bibliography of printed OFBs.
- **DES** — indexed data-entry projects: Verlustlisten 1. Weltkrieg,
  Adressbücher, civil registers of some cities.
- **Historische Adressbücher**, **Grabstein-Projekt**
  (grabsteine.genealogy.net), **Familienanzeigen**
  (familienanzeigen.genealogy.net), **Verlustlisten 1. WK**
  (verlustlisten.de), **Totenzettel**, **FOKO** (who researches which surname
  where), **DigiBib** (digitised genealogical literature), and the
  **Metasuche** (meta.genealogy.net) across these databases — use the
  Metasuche first.
- Mailing lists/forums by region — good for local parish-affiliation questions.

Regional societies add their own holdings, e.g. **WGfF** (Westdeutsche
Gesellschaft für Familienkunde) for Rhineland — Totenzettel collection,
publications of transcribed church books; **Roland zu Dortmund**; **AGoFF** for
former eastern territories.

## 5. Place resolution

- **Meyers Orts- und Verkehrslexikon 1912** (meyersgaz.org) — for every place
  in the German Empire: Standesamt, parishes, district, court. Searchable with
  wildcards; essential for places now in Poland, Russia, Lithuania, France,
  Denmark, Belgium, Czechia.
- **GOV** for changes over time and today's name.
- **Kartenmeister** (east of Oder–Neisse), historical maps (Messtischblätter,
  Tranchot/Müffling for the Rhineland, Urkataster).

## 6. Archives and finding aids

- **Archivportal-D / Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek** — cross-archive search of
  finding aids; DDB **Zeitungsportal** for digitised newspapers.
- **State archives:** e.g. Landesarchiv NRW — Abteilung Rheinland (Duisburg)
  and Abteilung Ostwestfalen-Lippe (Detmold, responsible for genealogy for all
  of Westphalia and Lippe) hold the Personenstandsarchive: civil-register
  duplicates, Zivilstandsregister and church-book duplicates, much of it
  digitised on archive.nrw.de (partly via FamilySearch). Every Land has
  an equivalent; search its online finding aid (e.g. Arcinsys in Hessen,
  Niedersachsen, Schleswig-Holstein).
- **Municipal archives (Stadt-/Gemeinde-/Kreisarchiv):** civil registers past
  the access period, Melderegister/Einwohnermeldekartei, Hausbücher,
  Adressbücher, Bürgerbücher, Totenzettel. Example Düsseldorf: civil records
  from 1798 (left bank) / 1810 (right bank), church-book copies 1603–1809,
  address books from 1844; Meldeunterlagen are searched by staff only, for a
  fee; proof of justified interest needed for restricted data.
- **Bundesarchiv** — military personnel records and the holdings of the former
  Deutsche Dienststelle (WASt), integrated in 2019; party and state records
  (Berlin-Lichterfelde); Lastenausgleichsarchiv Bayreuth (expellees' property
  claims, with village-level detail).
- **Arolsen Archives** — Nazi persecution, forced labour, displaced persons;
  large free online archive.
- **Volksbund Gräbersuche online** — war dead; **DRK-Suchdienst** — missing
  persons and expellees.
- **Emigration:** Hamburg passenger lists (Staatsarchiv Hamburg; indexed on
  Ancestry); Bremen lists mostly destroyed — about 3,000 of originally over
  4,500 lists from 1920–1939 survive (passagierlisten.de); state-archive
  emigration files (Auswanderungsakten), plus arrival records abroad.
- **Former eastern territories:** Polish state archives
  (szukajwarchiwach.gov.pl), Geneteka and other Polish indexing projects,
  Evangelisches Zentralarchiv Berlin, Bischöfliches Zentralarchiv Regensburg,
  Standesamt I Berlin, Sächsisches Staatsarchiv Leipzig (former Zentralstelle
  für Genealogie holdings).

## 7. Writing to an archive or Standesamt

Give everything known — full names incl. all given names, dates/periods, place,
confession, spouse, occupation — and state plainly what is known vs. assumed;
archives say explicitly that this lets discrepancies surface and avoids repeat
requests. Ask for: copy of the complete entry, Signatur and page, fee estimate
before work starts. State your relationship where access periods apply. Expect
weeks to months; research by staff is charged by time regardless of result;
own research in the reading room is usually free apart from reproductions.

## 8. Legal framework (orientation, not legal advice)

- **Personenstandsgesetz (PStG)** — civil registers and access periods.
- **Archivgesetze** of Bund and Länder — Schutzfristen for archive material,
  typically 30 years after file closure and 10 years after death (or 100 years
  after birth if death unknown) for person-related files; details differ by
  Land and can be shortened on application.
- **Church data-protection rules** (KDG, DSG-EKD) govern access to recent
  church records.
- **Urheberrecht** — old records are public domain, but portals' terms of use
  and archives' reproduction rules govern publication of images; photos and
  texts in other users' trees are protected.
